Research

Selected research projects in political economy, comparative growth, and public-sector labor markets.

Does Education's Effect on Political Participation Vary Across Cultures?

Capstone Project, SAIS  ·  Supervisor: Prof. Filipe Campante  ·  Nov. 2025 – May 2026

  • Conducted cross-country analysis of World Values Survey Wave 7 microdata to examine heterogeneous effects of education on political participation.
  • Estimated instrumental-variable models (2SLS), using language spoken at home as an instrument to address endogeneity.
  • Cleaned and merged multi-country data and documented a reproducible R workflow for analysis and figures.

GDP Convergence: Empirical Cases from China, the US, Japan, and South Korea

Course Research Project, SAIS  ·  Supervisor: Prof. Jaime Marquez  ·  Aug. 2025 – Nov. 2025

  • Used IMF World Economic Outlook data and autoregressive time-series models to examine comparative GDP-per-capita convergence paths.
  • Evaluated alternative quadratic and logarithmic specifications, moving-average adjustments, and robustness checks; interpreted results in relation to productivity and structural change.

Who Suffers the Most from Government Layoffs: Case Study of San Francisco

Course Research Project, LKYSPP  ·  Supervisor: Prof. Lawrence Jin  ·  Feb. 2025 – Apr. 2025

  • Cleaned and restructured a 167,464-observation administrative salary dataset covering public-sector job categories in San Francisco.
  • Applied unsupervised clustering in Python to identify employee groups with differing salary structures and potential layoff vulnerability.
  • Constructed a vulnerability index and translated the descriptive findings into a policy-focused research memo.
